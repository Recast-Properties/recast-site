How the listing looks to a buyer

The house is vacant, unstaged, and painted a uniform flip-gray, and the April purchase at $301,000 sits right in the listing's own price history. None of that is disqualifying — but together it invites lowball anchoring, and the copy never counters with the house's actual story. The strongest cards are barely played: a full finished basement with wet bar and its own separate driveway and entrance (a genuine multi-generational / in-law setup, the hottest niche in the move-up market), three HVAC units, two fireplaces, a 9-rated high school, and an $8/month HOA.

The fix

  1. Highest leverageFix the bedroom problem.

    Have the agent verify whether any basement room has legal egress and a closet. If yes, amend the MLS to 4 BR — it instantly changes which searches the house appears in. If not, work the flex angle hard in remarks and photo captions ("lives like 4–5 bedrooms; private lower-level suite with separate entrance"). Do not claim a bedroom that doesn't qualify.

  2. This weekRe-cut the gallery.

    Delete both AI-twilight duplicates. Order: front elevation → foyer → great room → kitchen → primary suite → then the basement as its own labeled chapter ("Lower-level suite · separate entrance") → rear/utility last. Overseed or sod the patchy front slope (mid-September is the East TN seeding window) and reshoot the hero when it's green.

  3. This weekRewrite the remarks honestly.

    Drop "beautifully updated." Lead with what no competitor offers: "4,740 sqft under $115/sqft — least-expensive large home in East Brainerd this year; private lower-level suite with its own driveway; East Hamilton (9/10) zone; $100/yr HOA. Fresh paint, new carpet, new LVP throughout." Selling the finish gap as priced-in equity beats pretending it isn't there.

  4. Neutralize the flip discount.

    Attach a pre-listing inspection, receipts for the work done, and a one-year home warranty. The buyer who can see the $301K purchase needs a reason to believe the spread is condition-adjusted, not concealed.

  5. Hold the price.

    The day-13 cut to $529,900 was early — traffic was already healthy. Hold ≥30 days and track showings-per-week (ask the agent; portals can't see it) rather than views. If a move is ever needed, $514,900 crosses the $515K search band; nothing before day 35–40.

  6. Add a floor plan.

    Winding Glen's gallery ends with measured floor plans; this one should too. On a ranch-over-basement, the plan is what makes 4,740 sqft believable.

How the listing looks to a buyer

The listing's data trail compounds it: five cuts plus one mid-listing price increase (May 25, tied to the contract negotiation, but on the portals it just looks erratic), a pending that lasted six days, a visible January foreclosure purchase at $412,824 — and Zillow is quietly carrying two duplicate pages for this same house (one showing "off market, $547,600 est.", a Lot 119 stub showing $633,000), fragmenting its record. After 171 days the portals have stopped promoting it: 4.1 views/day versus Red Tail's 43. The remarks read as keyword-stuffed SEO copy ("Cleveland, TN" three times) rather than a pitch a buyer's agent would repeat aloud.

The fix

  1. Step zeroAutopsy the June contract.

    A six-day death is an inspection shock or a financing collapse. Get the terminated buyer's inspection report if their agent will share it, cure what killed the deal, and keep the receipts. Everything else below just sends more buyers into the same wall until this is done. It could not be verified from public data what the issue was — treat that as the first question for the agent.

  2. Before relaunchTake it off market and rehab the presentation.

    Patch/overseed the yard, then a full summer reshoot: hero angle that clears the tree, kitchen without plastic, light staging or quality virtual staging, keep the floor plans and the summer pool/clubhouse aerials. Nothing in the March ground set should survive.

  3. With agentRelaunch as a fresh event.

    Have the agent confirm RealTracs / River Counties rules on days-on-market reset after withdrawal. Even where DOM carries over, a relist with all-new media plus a price event fires the portal notifications a 171-day listing never gets. Ask Zillow (via the agent) to merge the two duplicate pages while it's off market.

  4. With agentRewrite the remarks as a value pitch.

    The honest story is strong: "Least-expensive home in gated Eagle Creek — last three same-size neighborhood sales: $577K–$600K. 0.29-acre fenced yard, 471 sqft private upstairs suite with full bath, pool/clubhouse/gate for $58/mo." Attach the comp sheet to the MLS. Cut the SEO filler.

  5. Hold $499,000 — spend on payment, not price.

    The number is already below every relevant comp; a sixth cut signals distress without adding buyers. A 2-1 rate buydown (~$10–12K) reads as ~$450–500/mo relief to the payment-driven buyer this price band actually contains, and costs less than the next cut.

  6. Mind the carry math.

    In at $412,825 (Dec 2025) plus the refresh; at $499,000 less ~7–8% selling costs the gross spread is roughly $50–55K before renovation and nine-plus months of taxes, HOA, utilities and capital. Each additional month is real erosion — which is the argument for doing the relaunch properly once, not for another cut.
